# Session log - 2026-09-27

The first session. The repo started empty.

## What was asked

A REAPER tool that analyses imported MIDI: chord progressions for a melody,
melodies for a chord progression. ReaScript, JSFX or CLAP, whichever suits.
ScaleView-for-Reaper and Starting-Blocks were pointed to as prior work, with
the REAPER user guide, API reference, JSFX reference and a music theory
library uploaded.

## What was built

A Lua ReaScript ([0001](../decisions/0001-a-reascript-not-a-jsfx-or-clap.md))
laid out like Starting Blocks: pure music files, a REAPER layer, a ReaImGui
window, and tests for each against mocks.

- `ms_theory` - ScaleView's keys and spelling, a key finder, and ScaleView
  Pro's chord reader copied unchanged ([0002](../decisions/0002-chords-named-by-scaleview-pro.md)).
- `ms_read` - melody or chords, the melody line, a progression cut into
  chords (block, strummed, arpeggiated, and with a walking bass).
- `ms_harmony` - chord progressions by fit and T-S-D movement, k-best search.
- `ms_melody` - motif-based melodies, drawn and scored.
- `ms_place` - reading items, inserting on a new track with the source's
  instrument ([0003](../decisions/0003-a-new-track-with-the-sources-instrument.md)),
  auditioning on a temporary track ([0004](../decisions/0004-audition-on-a-temporary-track.md)).

## What the tuning found

**Key finding on short tunes.** Plain Krumhansl-Kessler read Ode to Joy's
first phrase as E minor, a key without the F natural the phrase plays. Two
checks were added - notes outside the key, time on the tonic chord - and the
weights chosen over fifteen tunes. Every setting tried missed exactly one:

| outside | triad | ending | wrong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | 0.12 | Ode, Twinkle bar 1, Twinkle in D, Happy Birthday |
| 1.0 | 0.5 | 0.12 | Happy Birthday |
| 1.0 | 0.6 | 0.15 | Happy Birthday |
| **1.0** | **0.6** | **0.2** | **Ode (first phrase, a half cadence)** |
| 1.0 | 0.7 | 0.2 | Ode |

The chosen row misses the tune that ends off its tonic, which is the rarer
case; the miss is pinned in the test.

**Chord suggestions were restless.** Twinkle first came out
`I iii ii V vii iii V I`. Three changes, each measured on the demo tunes:
charging the rarer degrees (`COST_DEGREE`), rewarding a held chord rather
than penalising it, and doubling the weight of the tune's last note. After
them Twinkle's first suggestion is `I IV I IV I V I`, Ode to Joy at a chord
a bar is `I V I V` (its half cadence), and the minor tune is
`i iv V i iv i`.

**Speed.** 128 bars took 2.4s; replacing a sort with a k-way merge of lists
already in order made it 0.38s with the same answers.

## What broke, and what the tests missed

**Four tests passed on the first run and were worthless until broken on
purpose.** Removing the key's ending bonus failed nothing (no tune in the
suite needed it); nor did removing the outside-the-key check; a voicing
chosen with no regard to the chord before passed a "no voice jumps more than
a fifth" check; and melodies that were near-duplicates passed a check that
they were not identical. Each got a case that fails without the rule.

**The UI sweep never reached the chord options.** It walked every button in
one session, and an early click on *Chords* switched the view. It now clicks
each button from a fresh start and names what it reached.

**Two fixture mistakes looked like engine bugs.** A pop progression was
written with the chord's lowest note doubled as its bass, so A minor stood on
C and read, correctly, `Amin/C`. And a voicing test assumed "under the
melody" meant "under middle C"; it means under the notes sounding at the
time. Both were the test, not the code.

## Later: chord editing and Auto timing

After the first run in REAPER ("the window looks right and suggestions sound
good"), the user asked for multiple chords in one clip following the melody,
like Wingman by Mixed In Key. Mixed In Key's pages are blocked from the
sandbox, so Wingman was researched through search summaries only: chord
replacement, passing chords, splitting and removing chord regions, a choice
of how often the harmony changes. The tool already put a whole progression in
one clip; the user chose three of four offered features - Auto timing, split
with passing chords, swap one chord.

**Built:** Auto timing ([0006](../decisions/0006-auto-reads-half-bars.md))
and chord-by-chord editing ([0007](../decisions/0007-edit-one-chord.md)).

**Found along the way, and fixed:**

- **Minor keys were charged major keys' rarity.** The degree cost table was
  indexed by degree alone, so in A minor it treated G major as if it were a
  diminished vii. The minor tune at a chord a bar read `Amin Bdim Emin Amin`;
  now `Amin G Amin`.
- **A secondary dominant resolving was scored by its target's function**, so
  V7/IV -> IV read as a retrogression and splitting C before F gave Cmaj7,
  not C7. Fixing it made Colourful flood with secondary dominants until the
  resolution bonus came down from 0.6 to 0.2 (0.1 and 0 were too sparing; at
  0 the C7 split fails).
- **An unresolved secondary dominant turned up in Auto**, B7 going to A
  minor. It had been a cost, not a rule; it is a rule now, and none may end a
  progression.

**Tried and abandoned:** a beat-by-beat Auto. Measured at five cost
settings; the search changed chord on most beats at every one of them. The
record is in 0006.

**Test gaps found by breaking things on purpose:** splitting onto the nearest
beat was never exercised, because every chord split had an even number of
beats - a three-beat case now covers it. Clicking editor chords by label hit
a same-named alternative instead; the UI test finds them by position.
